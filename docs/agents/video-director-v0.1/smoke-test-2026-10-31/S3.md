**Format (working title "This Week in [Product]")**

- **Structure:**

  - **Real product screen captures** as proof. Never mock-ups, never invented screens.

- Each language gets its own voice track, lip sync and captions.
- One decision is open: if your product's interface exists in Spanish and German, we capture each language's UI. If it doesn't, we show the English UI with translated callouts.

**Planning depth**

**What matters most:** first, that viewers clearly understand what's new and why it matters. Second, technical quality: a stable twin, believable lip sync in all three languages, legible text. Third, the artistic side, which comes mainly from a recognizable, consistent frame.

## The format: "This Week in Loomly"

**What it's for:** customers learn what shipped and use it. Success means they understand the update after one viewing, then click through to try it.

**Planning depth:** standard for the format, which we build once (twin, graphics system, template). Light for each episode after that. The fidelity risks are your face and voice in three languages, and real interfaces.

**Episode structure (60–90 s, master in 16:9)**

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Sting | ~4 s | Title, episode number, this week's top headline. Short sound logo |
| Host intro | ~8 s | Your twin greets viewers. Lower third with name and "AI-generated presenter" |
| Updates ×2–3 | ~20 s each | Twin line (what changed, why it matters to an agency) → real screen capture with callouts → benefit card |
| Close | ~10 s | What's next (only items you've cleared to announce) + one CTA |
| End card | ~4 s | Logo, link, subscribe, disclosure |

**Creative direction**
- **Tone:** a founder talking to customers she knows. Warm, direct, slightly informal, no hype. Every update answers "what does this save me?"
- **Pacing:** progressive. Calm twin lines, then quicker screen sections, then cards that hold long enough to read.
- **Twin framing:** medium shot with your head and shoulders centred, with room on both sides. That way the 9:16 cut crops cleanly and graphics can sit beside you in 16:9. The camera is static. The movement comes from the edit and the graphics.
- **Look:** Loomly's brand palette and type. I need your brand kit. A simple, light setting close to your recording's environment. Matching the recording is safer than inventing a studio.
- **Graphics system:** code-built and reused every week. Title sting, lower thirds, callout boxes, benefit cards, end card. Text is exact and swapped per language, with layouts that leave room for longer German text.
- **Screen captures:** real workflows recorded in the demo workspace, with slow zooms to the click or result that matters. One idea per capture.
- **Sound:** one short sound logo, a light music bed ducked under your voice, soft UI sounds on clicks and on cards appearing. Same palette every week.
- **Avoid:** mock-up screens, invented numbers, testimonials, any other brands or logos in the frame, and the twin "demonstrating" with its hands.

**Proposal for you to decide on:** say it on camera. In episode 1 the twin says something like: *"Quick note: this is my AI twin. I write every word, and it lets me talk to you in English, Spanish and German every week."* That's honest disclosure, and it turns a possible trust problem into part of the show. I'd keep a one-line reminder in the lower third in every episode either way.

**What stays fixed and what can change**

| Dimension | Latitude | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Your face, voice, wardrobe, setting | preserve | consent record + reference recording |
| Product UI | preserve (real captures only) | Loomly product |
| Claims | preserve (changelog only; anything else unknown) | changelog |
| Graphics system, sound logo | preserve once approved | your format approval |
| Number of updates, episode length | bounded (2–3 updates, 60–90 s) | format |
| Hook, angle of each update, small framing changes | open | |

**Localization**
- English is written first. Spanish and German are adapted, not translated literally. Feature names stay as they appear in the UI.
- Each language gets a native-speaker review before you see it. It's your voice saying things you can't check yourself.
- Timing is flexible in the twin lines only. Captures and cards keep fixed lengths, so all three versions share one edit.

**Weekly rhythm (proposal)**
1. Changelog to English script.
2. Spanish and German adaptations, then native review.
3. You approve the scripts.
4. Production runs against an approved scope.
5. Independent review, then you accept and publish.

Each week needs its own scope approval. A standing weekly scope with a fixed cap is possible later, but that's a separate decision for you.
