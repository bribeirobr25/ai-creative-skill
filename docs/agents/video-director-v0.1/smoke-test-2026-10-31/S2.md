What we can take is why that kind of spot works:

- **Restraint.** A dark, still world with one element moving.
- **The reveal comes from physics.** Water pulling off metal and crystal shows the product.
- **Patience.** The watch stays hidden longer than feels comfortable, then holds still once it's revealed.
- **Sound carries the luxury.** There's almost no music, so you hear every sound the water makes.

Here is a draft direction built on those principles, for you to react to.

## Draft direction: "Surface"

**Concept.** A single drop falls onto black, still water. The rings it makes turn into the circles of your watch's dial and bezel. The watch then rises through the surface, and the water draining off it is the reveal. The transitions are linked by shape: ripple, then dial, then watch.

**Tone.** Quiet, exact and confident, with a contemplative rhythm.

**Storyboard sketch (15 s)**

| # | Time | What happens | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3 s | Black. One drop lands on still water in macro, and the rings spread. | Hook: stillness and sound |
| 2 | 3–6 s | The rings dissolve into the concentric details of the dial, seen distorted under the surface. We only see part of the watch. | Tease: hint at the product without showing it |
| 3 | 6–10 s | Caustic light moves across the case under water and the surface starts to lift. | Build: material, light, anticipation |
| 4 | 10–13 s | The watch breaks the surface. Water sheets off the crystal in one pull and leaves it clear. | Reveal |
| 5 | 13–15 s | The watch holds in a still three-quarter view, then the end card appears with brand and launch date. | Payoff: hold long enough to register |

**Sound.** Low room tone, then the drop, a sub-bass swell under the build, and a water accent at the reveal. Then silence, and one closing sound from the watch itself. That last sound has to match the real movement: a sweep for a mechanical watch, a tick for quartz.

**Medium.** Hybrid. The water and macro shots are photoreal, built from approved stills of your watch. The end-card text and logo go in the graphics layer, never inside the generated footage.

**Planning depth: standard, with extra care on the product.** It's one product across five shots, but the fidelity risk is high. Small dial text, the hands and the logo are what generation most often gets wrong, and water refraction makes that harder.

**Fixed and free**
- **Fixed:** the watch's shape, finish, dial layout, text and logo, exactly as the real product.
- **Within your brand's range:** the palette and grade.
- **Open:** how the water behaves, the light, and the camera inside each shot.
- **Avoid:**
  - recognisable shots, framing, grade or music from the reference ad;
  - any other brand's marks;
  - anything that suggests more water resistance than the watch really has.
