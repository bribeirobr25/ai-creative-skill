# Creative direction: "Pippa Russet, Woodland Detective" (working title)

## Summary
- **Objective:** a pilot that makes 4–7-year-olds want to solve mysteries alongside Pippa, and leaves parents feeling it's a kind, smart show worth putting on.
- **Approach:** a "fair-play" mystery. Every clue is shown on screen before Pippa uses it. The case is never solved by catching a bad guy. It's solved by looking closer and understanding someone. Pippa makes one honest mistake per episode and owns it.
- **Why:** with ages 4–7, the youngest are still learning to read. They solve with their eyes, and they love being one step ahead of the hero. The kindness ending makes the show safe and warm without being preachy.

**Planning depth: deep.** She's a recurring character in several locations, on a series path, and redrawing is expensive. That's why the character sheet gets locked before anything else.

## Pippa: character proposal (needs your approval)
- **Who she is:** a young fox, about 8 in "story years", a little older than the viewers so they look up to her. She's curious and observant, and she asks lots of questions.
- **Her flaw:** she sometimes jumps to a conclusion too fast. That gives each episode its red herring, and it lets her model saying "I was wrong, I'm sorry."
- **Design:** a russet-orange fox with a cream chest and tail tip. She wears a moss-green scarf and carries a leaf-shaped notebook where she sketches each clue. The notebook is her signature prop, and it doubles as the recap device: kids see all the clues collected in one place. Her silhouette should be readable at a glance: big ears, an oversized tail, a slightly large head.
- **"Clue moment":** her ears perk, her tail twitches and a soft chime plays. The same cue repeats every episode, so kids learn to spot clues themselves.
- **Catchphrase (draft):** *"Look closer, think kinder."*
- **Sidekick:** Bram, a small, eager badger who asks the questions the audience would ask. He gives Pippa someone to explain things to, so we don't need narration.
- **World:** Mossbury, a woodland town of tree-root houses, a mill stream and a market square.

## Story bible seed (rules for every episode, for your approval)
1. Every clue appears on screen before Pippa explains it.
2. No villains. Mysteries come from misunderstandings, accidents or someone needing help.
3. Little peril, nothing frightening. Tension comes from curiosity, not danger.
4. Pippa makes one mistake per episode and makes it right.
5. Mysteries stay small and local: a missing thing, a strange noise, a puzzling change.
6. The episode ends with the catchphrase and a moment of the town together.

## Pilot outline: "The Case of the Missing Honey Cake" (about 3:00)

| Time | Beat | What happens | What the viewer gains |

| 0:00–0:15 | Hook | Mrs. Oakley, the owl baker, sets a honey cake on her windowsill to cool for tonight's Lantern Festival. A rustle, the camera turns back, and the cake is gone. | The mystery, in pictures alone |

The story arc runs from warm late afternoon to lantern-lit evening, so the ending is also the visual payoff.

## Creative direction
- **Tone:** warm, curious, gently funny. Never sarcastic, never scary.
- **Pacing:** calm and clear for this age group. Hold each clue long enough to look at it (about 2–3 seconds). Repeat key information once. Use a steady rhythm rather than fast cutting, and pick up the pace a little toward the reveal.
- **Camera:** mostly at the characters' eye level, with simple, stage-like staging and clear silhouettes against the backgrounds. Every clue gets the same close-up insert. Cuts stay gentle, with minimal camera moves.
- **Light and atmosphere:** golden late-afternoon light that moves into dusk with glowing lanterns. Shadows are soft and plum-coloured, never dark or menacing.
- **Emotional beats:** excitement (case!), worry (Rowan hurt), "aha" (the prints), tenderness (Bea and Grandpa), joy (lanterns).
- **Sound:** gentle woodland ambience (stream, birds, leaves). The investigation motif uses light woodwinds and pizzicato. The clue chime is identical every episode. Footsteps and paper sounds come from the notebook. Music swells softly for the lanterns.
- **Voices (synthetic):** warm, very clear diction and a slightly slower pace than adult speech, with a distinct voice for each character. Use only voices whose licence allows commercial use on YouTube. No cloned real voices without consent, and no imitating famous actors or existing characters.

## Look
- **Characters:** clean 2D with soft, slightly rounded line and flat colour with one shade level, built for a reusable rig.
- **Backgrounds:** a painterly gouache or paper texture, so the world feels handmade while the characters stay crisp.
- **Palette:** russet orange (Pippa), moss and fern greens, cream, honey gold, plum shadows. Warm and moderately saturated, never neon.
- **Framing:** 16:9 main version. Keep key action near the centre so moments can be re-cut later as vertical Shorts (optional, see below).
- **Avoid:** any resemblance to existing animated foxes in silhouette, outfit or colour blocking. No real brands, and no text clues the youngest viewers can't read.

## What's fixed and what's free
- **Preserve (once you approve it):** Pippa's design, her name, the notebook, the clue cue and chime, the catchphrase, the story bible rules.
- **Bounded:** expressions and poses stay within the character sheet. Background colours stay within the palette. Episodes run about 3–4 minutes.
- **Open:** each episode's mystery, the locations, guest characters, the music variations.
