## User

I need a TikTok ad for my serum, like those girls who review products. Show wrinkles disappearing in 7 days, before and after. I want it to go viral.

## Director

I can make you a creator-style TikTok ad that feels native in the feed. First, though, there's one part of the brief I have to change, because it decides the whole concept.

**I can't plan a generated before and after of wrinkles disappearing.** If I invent faces showing a 7-day result, the ad shows proof that doesn't exist. Viewers would read it as real, and so would TikTok's ad review and consumer regulators. It's also the fastest way to get a beauty ad rejected or your account flagged. TikTok's rules for cosmetics are strict about before and after images and exaggerated results. Check the current policy for your market, but expect "wrinkles disappear in 7 days" to be refused even if you film it with real people. For the same reason I won't plan an AI "girl" who says the serum worked on her. A generated customer isn't a customer.

Here's what we can do instead, and it can still be very strong.

**Direction: "7-Day Honest Diary"** (9:16, about 20 seconds, creator review style)
1. **Hook, 0 to 2s:** an extreme close-up of a serum drop landing on a fingertip, then the creator to camera: "I used this every morning for 7 days. Honest review."
2. **Her concern, 2 to 6s:** what she wanted to try it for, in her own words.
3. **Routine, 6 to 12s:** texture, how it goes on, how fast it absorbs. This is product truth, so it's safe and very watchable.
4. **Day 1 and Day 7, 12 to 17s:** same window, same light, same angle, no filters, no retouching. The day labels and any results claim go in as text in the edit, with exact wording.
5. **Call to action, 17 to 20s.**

Step 4 only exists if it's **real footage of a real person who agreed to it**. Otherwise we drop it and the ad leads with the routine and how the serum feels, which is often what makes these videos work anyway.

**What I need from you (this decides which version we make):**
1. **What's actually proven?** Do you have a study or test results for this serum? Send me the exact wording and numbers, for example "X% of N people saw reduced appearance of fine lines after 7 days". Without that, the ad makes no results claim at all.
2. **Real person or AI presenter?** A real creator or customer filming the 7 days on their phone gives you the strongest and most honest version. An AI presenter is possible, but it would be labelled as AI and would only show the product and routine, never personal results.
3. **Product material:** clear photos of the bottle from several angles with a readable label, plus the brand name, the country you're advertising in and the language.

About going viral: nobody can promise that, and I'd be misleading you if I did. What we can control is a hook that stops the scroll in the first second, a format that feels native to TikTok, and a single clear message. Once we have results, we test two or three hooks against each other.

Answer those three and I'll send the full direction and shot plan, plus what it would take to produce it.

## User

Is that enough to make something good? 😊

## Director

Yes, that's enough to make something good. Filming it yourself is the best option, not just the cheapest one.

**Film it yourself rather than using AI**
- **You have something AI can't fake.** You make every bottle yourself and you sell at markets, and that's a real story. On TikTok, a maker showing their own process tends to come across as genuine. I can't promise how it will perform, but you're the most credible face this brand has.
- **AI isn't really cheaper.** Every generated attempt costs money and many get thrown away. It would also have to be labelled as AI, and it couldn't honestly say anything about using your serum.
- **Your single photo is fine for this.** It only shows the front, so it wouldn't be enough for an AI to reproduce the bottle accurately. You'd just get a wrong label. But you have the real bottle, so the camera does that job.

**Without a study, here's what we say and what we leave out**
You can say what you make, what goes into it, how it feels and how people react at your stall. Don't mention wrinkles, anti-ageing, "results in 7 days" or anything else that promises a change to skin. In the UK the ASA expects every claim in an ad to be backed up. Customer reviews count as claims too, so if a real customer agrees to let you use their words, choose quotes about how it feels or smells, not about results. The ASA publishes free guidance on cosmetics advertising, and it's worth a 10-minute read.

**Concept: "Made by me, in my kitchen"** (a founder ad for Dewdrop)
9:16, about 25 seconds, filmed on your phone, edited in a free app.
**Why it fits:** it turns "small and handmade" into the selling point, it costs nothing, and every shot is real.

**Planning depth: light.** It's one person and one product, and if a shot goes wrong you just film it again for free. A written shot plan is enough, so no storyboard.

**Shot plan**
| # | Time | What we see | What you say (rough) | Text on screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | **Hook.** The bottle held close to the lens, then your face appears behind it | Pick one hook (below) | none |
| 2 | 2–8s | Your hands making a batch: pouring, mixing, filling, putting on labels | "Every bottle is made by me, in small batches." | "Small-batch · handmade" |
| 3 | 8–13s | Close-ups of the real ingredients | Say what's in it and why you chose it, and only what's true | Exact ingredient names |
| 4 | 13–20s | One drop on a fingertip, then putting it on your own face by a window | How it feels, for example "light, sinks in fast", only if true for you | none |
| 5 | 20–24s | Your market stall: real people, real bottles on the table | "This is where most of you find me." | A real review, used with permission (about how it feels, not results) |
| 6 | 24–27s | You holding the bottle, smiling | "Dewdrop. Link in my bio." | "Dewdrop" + where to buy |

**Hooks to try** (film all three and post them as separate versions. Which works best is a guess until you see your own numbers.)
- A: "I make every bottle of this serum in my kitchen. Here's how."
- B: "People at my market stall keep asking what's in this. So here it is."
- C: Start straight on the drop falling onto your fingertip with sound only, then speak.

**Look and sound**
- Daylight from a window, with no beauty filters and no smoothing. Warm, simple, real.
- A clean corner of your kitchen. Clear away other brands' packaging and logos.
- Sound matters: get the drip, the cap clicking, the bottles clinking. Film your voice in a quiet room with the phone close to you.
- For music, use only tracks from TikTok's commercial music library if you promote the post. Normal chart songs aren't cleared for ads. Check that rule in the app.

**Filming tips**
- Use the back camera, film upright, wipe the lens first, and shoot each part 3 times.
- Film every shot once **without** text so you can reuse it later for new hooks, Instagram Reels or a sign at your stall.
- Add all text in the editing app rather than writing it on anything in the shot, so the spelling stays exact.

**Assumptions to confirm**
- The ingredient list and any feel words ("light", "fast-absorbing") are true for your product.
- As you already sell it, the serum has its UK safety assessment and correct labelling in place.
- Any customer whose face or words appear has said yes.

**What matters most here:** first, clear and honest communication. Second, technical basics: the label is readable, and the sound is clean. Artistic polish comes third.

**Proposed plan (version 1, nothing happens until you say yes)**
- **Cost:** £0. You film and edit, and no paid tools are needed.
- **Fixed:** the real bottle and label, your real process, true claims only.
- **Can vary:** which hook, the order of shots, music from the cleared library.
- **Never:** before and after shots, results claims, filters, invented reviews.
- **Risks:** shaky or dark footage (fix it with window light and by resting the phone on something), or a claim slipping into the voiceover (check it against the "never" list).

Approve this version, or tell me what to change. Choosing which hook you like best is the main decision.
