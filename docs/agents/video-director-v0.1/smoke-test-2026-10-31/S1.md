## User

## Director

Happy to. A spending report is a good fit for motion graphics: numbers, categories and charts look great when they move, and they can be kept exact. Below is a first direction built on stated assumptions, then the few things I need from you before I turn it into a full plan.

**What I'm assuming (please confirm or correct)**
- **Format:** an Instagram Reel, 9:16 vertical, about 15 seconds (anything from 10 to 20 works), 1080×1920. It needs to make sense with the sound off.
- **Audience and goal:** your existing users, so they find out the report exists and open it. If you want new users to download the app, the hook and the call to action change.
- **Sound:** punchy music and crisp sound effects that land on each animation beat. No voice-over.
- **"Make it pop":** bold colour, fast kinetic typography, snappy cuts on the beat and one clear payoff moment, all in your brand colours rather than generic neon.

**Two directions to choose from**

**A · "Chaos to clarity"** (my recommendation)
A month of spending bursts onto the screen as dozens of small transaction chips (coffee, rent, rides). They snap into categories, the categories grow into bars, and the bars settle into your actual report screen. The message is: your messy month, sorted for you.

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.5 s | Hook: an explosion of transaction chips | "Where did October go?" |
| 1.5–5 s | Chips sort into colour-coded categories in rhythm | Category names |
| 5–9 s | The bars turn into the real report on a phone | Total for the month |
| 9–12 s | One insight pops out with a zoom and a sound accent | e.g. "Food: top category" |
| 12–15 s | Logo and call to action, held long enough to read | "Your report is ready. Open the app." |

**B · "The receipt"**
One long paper receipt unrolls toward the camera, folds itself up and turns into the clean report. It feels more tactile and premium, and a bit less high-energy than A.

**How I'd build it:** code-based motion graphics, so every number, label and colour comes out exactly right. Video models still tend to garble on-screen text and figures, which is the last thing you want in a finance video. I'm treating this as a lean "standard" plan. There are no characters and only one setting. The main risk is accuracy: the interface and the numbers have to be right.

**What I need from you**
1. **Your brand:** the app name, logo, colours and font, or a brand guide if you have one.
2. **The real report screen:** screenshots or a short screen recording of the actual monthly report. I won't invent screens. Please use a demo account with made-up figures, never a real customer's data.
3. **Approved wording:** if you want any claim in the video (e.g. "spend smarter", "save more"), tell me which ones are approved. Otherwise I'll stick to describing what the feature does.
4. **Your pick:** A or B. Also tell me whether this is an organic post or a paid ad, and whether you'll want other versions (4:5 feed, Stories). If you do, I'll keep text on a separate layer so we can resize later without rebuilding.

Once I have these, I'll come back with the finished creative direction, style frames, the shot-by-shot storyboard and a proposed production scope with a cost estimate. Nothing gets produced or paid for until you approve that scope.

## Director

Thanks, that settles most of it. One item needs attention before anything else: the 30% claim.

**"Users save 30% on average"**
I can't put this in the video until it has a source behind it. A savings figure in an ad meant to drive downloads is the kind of claim ad platforms and consumer-protection rules expect you to be able to back up. If it turns out to be wrong, you have the problem, not just the video. Please send:
- **Where the number comes from:** your own user data, a survey, or something else.
- **What "save 30%" measures:** 30% less spending than before? 30% more put into savings? Compared with what, and over how long?
- **Who it covers:** all users, or only active users after X months?

Once I have that, I'll write the claim exactly as the data supports it, with a short line of fine print (e.g. "Based on N active users, Jan–Sep 2026") held on screen long enough to read. Until then, the hook has a slot for the claim and a ready fallback that needs no proof: **"Where did your money go?"** The edit works either way.

**Your other answers, and what they change**
- **Goal is downloads:** the audience is now people who don't have the app. The video has to explain what Pennywise is within a few seconds, and it ends on "Get Pennywise" with app-store badges. Please confirm which stores you're on. I'll only add "free" if that's true.
- **Colours:** teal is the base and coral is the "pop" accent, used for the claim, the insight and the CTA. Please send exact colour codes if you have them.
- **Font:** I'll use a free, openly licensed bold sans with fixed-width numbers, such as Manrope or Inter, so the counters don't jitter. This is a placeholder for the video, not a brand decision.
- **Name:** Pennywise is also a famous horror character. To keep the two apart, I'll keep clowns, red balloons and drains out entirely, and make sure coral always reads as warm and friendly rather than blood-red.
- **Screenshots:** yes, please send all three. A screenshot only shows what's in it, so the animation will move, zoom and highlight those three screens. It won't invent scrolling or screens we can't see. They need to show demo figures only, at full phone resolution.

## Creative direction: "Chaos to clarity"

**Concept:** your money is a mess of little transactions. Pennywise turns it into one clear monthly picture.
**Message:** "See where your money goes, every month." Plus the 30% claim, once it has a source.
**Tone:** confident, upbeat, quick. A little playful, never preachy about spending.
**Pacing:** explosive opening, rhythmic middle, and the call to action held still long enough to read.
**Camera:** flat graphic space with fast push-ins and match cuts: chip → bar → screen. The phone tilts slightly in 3D for depth.
**Sound:** bright, percussive licensed track. Each chip landing gets a tick, the bars rising get a whoosh, the insight gets a soft "pop". Ends on a clean accent under the logo.
**Avoid:** invented screens or features, real people or testimonials, other companies' logos on the transaction chips (generic labels like "Coffee" or "Rent" only), horror imagery, unapproved claims.

**Planning depth: standard (lean).** There are no characters and one setting. The risk is accuracy: real interface, exact numbers, a claim. So I'm planning style frames and a key-frame storyboard, but no full treatment.

**What's fixed and what's free**
| Element | Latitude | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| Report screens, figures, claim wording | Preserve | Your screenshots and the claim's source |
| Logo, teal and coral | Preserve | Your brand |
| Font | Bounded: open-licensed bold sans | Your instruction |
| Length | Bounded: 13–17 s | My assumption |
| Motion style, transitions, chip design | Open | — |

## Storyboard (9:16, about 15 s)

| # | Time | Purpose | Action and camera | On-screen text (rendered exactly in the graphics layer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2 s | Hook | Dozens of teal transaction chips burst toward the camera. A coral "30%" counter slams to the centre | "Pennywise users save 30% on average" + fine print, *or the fallback* "Where did your money go?" |

| 5 | 12–15 s | Download | Logo resolves from the last chip. Store badges appear. Holds still for at least 2 s | "Get Pennywise" + store badges |

Text stays clear of Instagram's top and bottom interface areas. Every frame works with the sound off.

**Reuse:** text, logo and screenshots each sit on their own layer, so a 4:5 feed version or a Stories cut is a resize, not a rebuild. Say the word and I'll add those to the scope.

**What matters most:** commercial first (a clear hook, a substantiated claim, a call to action that gets downloads), technical second (exact numbers, readable text, a true interface), artistic third.

- **Steps:** build 3 style frames → animatic → final animation → sound design and music → export.
- **Attempts:** up to 2 rounds on style frames and 2 on the animation.
- **Build method:** code-based motion graphics plus a stock-music library licence. No video-model generation is needed, so there are no per-second generation fees.
- **Cost:** the only expected out-of-pocket cost is the music licence. You set the cap; I'd keep it to one track.
- **Risks:** the claim isn't substantiated yet; screenshots may be low-resolution or contain real data; app-store and "free" wording isn't confirmed.
- **Gates:** nothing is produced or paid for until you approve this exact version. Any change makes a v2 that needs its own approval. An independent reviewer checks the final video before it's accepted.
