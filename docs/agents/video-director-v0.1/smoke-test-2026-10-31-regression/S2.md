## Director

I can give your launch that feeling, but not the same ad. If the concept, shots and look are close enough that viewers recognise the original, your watch will remind people of the other brand, and you take on legal risk as well. What I can do is work out why that kind of film works and build an original version around your watch.

**Why "watch rising from water" usually works:**
- The reveal is slow and controlled: darkness, then the surface, then the watch breaking through.
- Light on wet metal and glass does most of the storytelling.
- Sound carries the tension: muffled underwater, then a crisp break at the surface.
- The final shot is held long enough to read the dial and the name.

**Two original starting points (you choose):**
- **A · Depth:** dark water and a single shaft of light. The watch rises, droplets slide off in slow motion, and it ends on your logo.
- **B · Surface:** an extreme close-up of one drop landing on the crystal. The ripple pulls back to reveal the whole watch, and the water settles into your logo.

**What I need from you:**
1. Photos of your watch from the front, side and back, plus the clasp, on a plain background. The reveal turns the watch, and a front-only photo can't guarantee what the other sides look like.
2. Which ad you mean, or a link. I'll pull out its principles and list what we must avoid copying.
3. Where it will run (vertical social, 16:9 or both) and the end line or logo.

**What I'm assuming (tell me if any is wrong):** 15 seconds, four to five shots, and logo and text added in the edit, not left to the video model. If the film suggests the watch is water-resistant, we'll only use your official rating.

**Shot plan, 15 s**

| # | Time | Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3 s | Black. One drop falls and lands on the crystal. Macro, dial out of focus. | Hook |
| 2 | 3–6 s | A ripple crosses the crystal, light runs along it, focus shifts to the hands and markers | The first look at the dial |
| 3 | 6–10 s | Pull back to the whole watch resting on dark wet stone under a thin layer of water | The reveal |
| 4 | 10–12.5 s | The water goes still, with a clean reflection of the case | The pause, time to read the dial |
| 5 | 12.5–15 s | The ripple settles into the Halden logo, held for at least 2 s | Brand |

- **Sound:** silence, then one clear drop, a soft rising tone under shots 2 and 3, and quiet on the logo. No voice. I'm assuming no music; tell me if you want a track.
- **Keep exact:** case shape, dial, hands, strap, colours (all from your renders), and the logo.
- **Can vary:** how the water moves, the angle of the light, how dark the background is.
- **Avoid:** hands or people, diving imagery (the 100 m rating supports water, not depth), other brands' marks, and on-screen text other than the logo.

**Planning depth: standard, kept lean.** With one product and no characters, a full moodboard isn't needed. Your renders plus the first key frames will set the look. The detail has to be strict, though: dial text and reflections on metal and glass are where generated shots usually go wrong.
