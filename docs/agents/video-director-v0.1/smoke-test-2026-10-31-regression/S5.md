## User

I want to make a 2D animated kids' series about a fox detective. Can you direct the pilot episode, about 3 minutes?

## Director

Happy to direct this. First, something you should know: a series goes beyond the ad and brand videos I'm mainly set up for. A story lead would normally own the characters and the series' rules. There isn't one yet, so anything I write about the fox and their world is a draft for you to confirm. It isn't settled until you do.

Three answers will get us started:

1. **Who is it for?** Roughly ages 3–5, 5–8 or 8–11? This decides how hard the mystery is, how fast it moves and how tense it can get.
2. **What do you already have?** A name, drawings, a world or a tone you love, or are we starting from scratch?
3. **What do you want first?** I suggest a pilot concept and story outline, plus a short draft of the characters and world. The visual style, storyboard and production plan come after you approve that.

**A choice to make early: how many characters come back every episode.** Each one has to be designed once and then drawn exactly the same in every episode, so the pilot commits the whole series. I'd start with the fox, one sidekick and two or three places, and give each episode one guest character.

**An idea for you to react to (not a decision):** kids see each clue a moment before the fox names it, so they can solve the case alongside the fox.

Nothing gets made or paid for at this stage. I plan, and production starts only after you approve a scope and a budget.

**Series rules (draft)**
- Nobody is a villain. Every mystery has a kind explanation.
- Clues are always fair. Each one is shown on screen a beat before the fox names it, with a short pause so kids can call it out.
- Three clues per episode, then the solve, then everyone helps.
- Nothing is scary. Small puzzles, warm endings.

**Recurring cast (kept small on purpose)**
- **The fox (placeholder "Fen"):** curious and gentle, notices tiny things. Catchphrase: "Let's look closer!" A simple shape with few colours and one signature item (a satchel), because every detail has to be drawn exactly the same in every episode.
- **The sidekick ("Pip," a mole):** can't see far but hears and smells everything. That makes the two of them a natural team, and it gives kids a second way to notice clues.
- **Places:** Fen's den with a clue board, the town square with its bell tower, and the woodland path.

**Pilot: "The Case of the Silent Bell" (about 3 minutes)**
- **0:00–0:20:** The town wakes up, but the morning bell goes *clunk*. Everyone is puzzled.
- **0:20–0:40:** Fen and Pip take the case.
- **0:40–1:10 · Clue 1:** An acorn shell at the foot of the tower. Kids see it first.
- **1:10–1:40 · Clue 2:** Tiny muddy paw prints going up the bell rope.
- **1:40–2:10 · Clue 3:** Pip hears a soft rustling up inside the bell.
- **2:10–2:40 · Solve:** A young squirrel (this episode's guest) hid her acorns in the bell because her tree hollow leaks.
- **2:40–3:00:** The town fixes her hollow. The bell rings. Fen says, "Let's look closer!"

**What this commits you to:** two characters and three places that every later episode reuses, plus one new guest per episode.

**Still to confirm before this is settled:** names, personalities, the rules, the cast size and the world. These normally belong to the story lead. Kids "solving alongside the fox" is an idea I'd want to test with a few children in your age group. It isn't proven yet. **Pilot outline: "The Case of the Silent Bell" (about 3 min, draft)**

1. **0:00–0:20 · Town square at dawn.** Someone pulls the bell rope and the bell goes *clunk*. Background townsfolk with no lines (so there are fewer characters to design) mutter, "It's broken! Who did it?"
2. **0:20–0:40 · Fen's den.** Pip bursts in: "Somebody broke the bell on purpose!" Fen: "Maybe. But we don't know yet. Let's look closer!"
3. **0:40–1:10 · Clue 1.** An acorn shell sits by the tower, held on screen for about 2 seconds before Fen sees it. Pip sniffs: "Fresh!" Then he guesses: "A big hungry bear!" Fen: "Could be… let's keep looking."
4. **1:10–1:40 · Clue 2.** Tiny muddy paw prints go up the rope, shown first and then named. Fen: "Someone small… who's good at climbing." Pip: "…So not a bear."
5. **1:40–2:10 · Clue 3.** Pip's ear twitches before he speaks, and the audience hears the rustle too. "Something's up there!" Fen: "Small, climbs, eats acorns…" Then a pause so kids can shout "a squirrel!"
6. **2:10–2:40 · The solve.** A squirrel (placeholder "Hazel") peeks out, worried. Pip starts: "You broke—" Fen, gently: "Can we ask you something? Why are your acorns in the bell?" Hazel: "My tree hollow leaks. The bell was the only dry place." Pip: "Oh. I thought you were being naughty."
7. **2:40–3:00 · Everyone helps.** The townsfolk patch her hollow and the bell rings clear. Fen tells the kids watching: "You spotted it first!" Then: "Let's look closer!"

**Still a draft until you confirm it:** Pip's trait, Hazel's name, townsfolk with no lines, and the solve-along idea. We still need to test the solve-along idea with a few children aged 4–7.

**Hazel (squirrel, guest)**
