## User

I need a TikTok ad for my serum, like those girls who review products. Show wrinkles disappearing in 7 days, before and after. I want it to go viral.

## Director

I can help you get to a strong TikTok ad. Two parts of this brief need a decision before I plan it.

**1. The "girl reviewing the product."** If that person is generated or hired to act, viewers will take her for a real customer, and I can't present it that way. A UGC-style ad still works with:
- **real customers or creators** who agree to appear and say what they actually experienced, or
- a clearly presented **brand creator or actor** who demonstrates the serum without claiming to be a customer.

**2. "Wrinkles gone in 7 days," before and after.** This is a result claim. It can only go in if you have evidence for it, such as a study, and real photos you have the rights to use. A generated before/after would be fake proof. TikTok also tends to restrict before/after skincare ads, so check its current ad policy before relying on one.

**Three ways to go:**
- **A. Real review:** real users, real routine, honest words. I'd plan the hook, script and shots.
- **B. Sensory ritual:** close-ups of the texture, the drop, application, the glow. No result claims. The serum is the star.
- **C. Backed before/after:** only if you have the evidence. It would use the exact approved wording and real photos.

I can't promise it goes viral. Nobody can. What I can do is design a hook that stops the scroll and a clear call to action.

**What I need from you:**
- Do you have evidence for the 7-day result?
- Are there real customers who would appear?
- Can you send product photos from all sides, with the label readable?
- Which way do you prefer: A, B or C?

## User

Oh, I don't have any study or anything like that, it's just what I've heard from people who use it! And no proper customers who'd go on camera, I don't think. I've only got one photo, the front of the bottle on my kitchen table. Option B sounds lovely though, let's do that one!

## Director

B it is. Here's the plan in short, with the details after.

**The idea: "The 15-second ritual."** Slow, close, satisfying shots of your serum: a drop forming, the texture on skin, the dewy finish in soft morning light. The sound carries a lot of it: the glass clink, the drip, fingertips pressing in. There's no fake reviewer and no promise about wrinkles. That keeps the ad honest, and it also stays away from TikTok's rules on skincare results.

**Two things from what you told me:**
- "What I've heard from people who use it" can't go in the ad as a statement, not even in a caption. It stays out unless you later have real reviews you're allowed to quote.
- One front photo isn't enough. The close-ups show the dropper, the cap, the sides and the serum itself, and a photo only secures what it shows. Without more photos the bottle may come out a different shape or with a garbled label.

**What I need from you:**
1. Photos of the bottle from the front, back, both sides and top, in daylight on a plain background, with the label readable. Also one of the dropper with serum in it, and one drop on the back of your hand, so the real colour and texture are captured.
2. The product name, and any words from the label you'd like on screen.
3. A rough budget.

**An option:** you could film shots 1 to 3 yourself on your phone. Real footage of the real serum is often the most convincing, and I'd give you a simple shot list.

---

**Details**

*Planning depth: light.* It's one short piece with one product, so a written shot plan is enough. The real risk is the bottle looking wrong, so Production first makes two or three still frames for you to check before any motion.

*Shot plan (vertical 9:16, about 15 s, sound on):*
1. **0–2 s, hook:** extreme close-up as a single drop gathers at the dropper tip and falls in slow motion, with the drip sound amplified.
2. **2–5 s:** the drop lands on a fingertip and spreads. Macro shot of the texture.
3. **5–9 s:** fingertips press the serum into a cheek, with soft side light. The face is mostly out of frame, and the person is never presented as a customer.
4. **9–12 s:** the cheek catches the window light. This shows the feel right after applying, not a result.
5. **12–15 s, hero:** the bottle in the same light, label sharp. The name and call to action are added as text in the edit, not by the video model.

*Look:* warm morning light, linen and stone surfaces, colours taken from your bottle and label.
*Sound:* no voice. Close-up sounds of the drop and the skin over a soft licensed music track.
*Keep fixed:* bottle shape, label, serum colour.
*Avoid:* before/after, any word about wrinkles or results, a reviewer persona, other brands' logos.
*Most important qualities:* technical (the product looks exactly right) and artistic (the sensory feel). The hook covers the commercial side.

*Assumptions to confirm:* 9:16, about 15 s, English, no voice-over, call to action "link in bio". If you'd like Instagram versions later, Production can keep the text on a separate layer so the ad can be re-cut without being remade.

*Good to know:* I can't promise it goes viral. The falling drop is built to stop the scroll, and "shorter performs better" is an untested idea, not a fact. TikTok asks for AI-generated content to be labelled, so check its current rules before posting. Nothing gets made or paid for until you approve a specific plan and budget.

NEXT STEP: When you send the photos, the label wording and a budget, I'd write the production handoff: a brief for each of the five shots, which photos each one uses, how many attempts, and the quantities Production needs to give a dated price. You'd approve that exact version before anything is made, starting with the still frames of the bottle for you to check.
