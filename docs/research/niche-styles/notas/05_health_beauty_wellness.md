# Health, Beauty Services & Wellness: Web Design Style, Layout & UI/UX by Niche

Scope: visual design, layout and UI/UX only (no copy, SEO or regulation). Niches: (a) medical practices/clinics, (b) dental, (c) psychologists/therapists/counselling, (d) hair salons, barbers, nail studios, aesthetic/cosmetic clinics (med spas), spas, (e) gyms, fitness, yoga/pilates, personal trainers, (f) physiotherapy and alternative/holistic wellness.

Research notes, Oct 2026. Most example-roundup sources are agency or builder blogs (Colorlib, Zarla, GlossGenius, Krishaweb, Seahawk, KIJO, Framer, Workee). They are good evidence of what sites currently look like (prototypicality), but they are not neutral usability research. Peer-reviewed or NN/g-grade evidence is marked as such. Anything labelled "Inference" or "Synthesis" is my own reasoning, not a sourced fact.

---

## 1. Visitor goals and primary conversion actions per niche, and how the UI supports them

### Takeaway
In every niche the main conversion is a booking action: book an appointment (medical, dental, physio, salon, spa), book a free consultation (therapy, aesthetics, orthodontics) or book a class or claim an intro offer (fitness, yoga, pilates). Current sites keep that action visible on every page, usually as a persistent header button. They often add a lower-commitment second path: phone, walk-ins, "not ready to book" info, a quiz, or a matching tool.

### Cited Findings
**Cross-niche / healthcare booking evidence**
- UK GP study (JMIR 2024, peer-reviewed; 1.33M GPPS survey responders from 2018–2019 plus 43 interviews). 45.11% of patients knew online booking existed and 15.61% had used it. Patients who rated the practice website "very easy to use" were far more likely to use online booking (OR 6.34 vs "not easy at all"). Use dropped sharply after age 75, and older patients often preferred the phone. Note that the data predates the pandemic. — [JMIR 2024, e51931](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e51931)
- Design implications from the same study: make the booking route visible and easy to find on the website, keep telephone access, and make sure enough online slots exist, including urgent ones. — [JMIR 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e51931)
- US Jarrard survey (1,034 adults, Mar–Apr 2024): 43% had never scheduled online. Their reasons were: provider doesn't offer it 22%, unfamiliar 21%, can't get an appointment soon enough 17%, platform not effective 8%, unsure what appointment type they need 7%, no computer/internet 4%, can't remember login 3%. — [Becker's Hospital Review, Jun 2024](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/innovation/why-patients-dont-use-online-scheduling)

**(a) Medical clinics**
- Framer's healthcare template roundup reports "booking as a core goal" across examples: prominent CTAs, mobile-friendly or on-page scheduling, prominent search bars on conversion-focused templates, and staff profiles meant to make booking less intimidating. — [Framer blog, healthcare website design examples](https://www.framer.com/blog/healthcare-website-design-examples/)
- Webflow's medical template FAQ lists the standard feature set as appointment booking, patient testimonials, health resources, video-consult integration, patient portals and real-time chat. — [Webflow medical templates](https://webflow.com/templates/category/medical-websites)

**(b) Dental**
- Recommended patterns: online booking reachable from every page rather than buried in Contact; emergency/urgent info immediately visible; price ranges and financing shown upfront. Beehive Dental keeps the booking form "never more than one click away" with the phone number visible throughout. 1-800-DENTIST uses the phone as the primary CTA. Diamond Braces leads with a prominent free-consultation offer. Smile Direct Club uses an assessment tool as its main hook. — [Krishaweb, 12 Best Dental Website Designs 2026](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-dental-website-designs/)

**(c) Therapy / counselling**
- Of 29 therapist sites reviewed (Colorlib, Oct 2026), the recurring conversions were a free 15-minute consultation (e.g., blwtherapy.com, coupleslearn.com, SR Psychological), an availability status banner ("accepting clients", "caseload full / referrals", "no waitlist", "immediate online openings"), and "fee one click away" or fee on the homepage. Some sites replace the contact form with a quiz or matching tool (theempoweringspace.com, srpsychologicalservices.com). Others use a search-led layout filtering by care type, location and insurance (deepeddypsychotherapy.com). Embedded scheduling comes from SimplePractice, Halaxy, Zencare or Psychology Today. — [Colorlib, therapist websites](https://colorlib.com/wp/therapist-websites/)

**(d) Salons, barbers, nails, spas, med spas**
- Barbers: most examples center one booking button. Imperium shows "Book Appointment" next to "Walk-ins welcome", Legends pairs Book Now with Shop Now, and Curbside Cuts promises booking "in 60 seconds". — [Zarla barber examples, updated Sep 2026](https://www.zarla.com/guides/barbershop-website-examples)
- Nail salons rely on third-party booking engines (Zenoti, Vagaro, Fresha, Square, Boulevard, Meevo). Memberships ($59–$99/mo at Nail Garden), gift certificates, loyalty programmes and press-on e-commerce are secondary conversions. — [Colorlib nail salon examples, Oct 2026](https://colorlib.com/wp/nail-salon-website-examples/)
- Spas: a prominent "Book" button is the norm on GlossGenius-built spa sites. Urban Nirvana uses a floating header with dropdown, a booking CTA and a back-to-top button. — [GlossGenius spa websites (2024)](https://glossgenius.com/blog/spa-websites)
- Med spas: booking is the visible primary action on most sites, often with a lower-commitment alternative. Revive offers a free consultation and Tribeca MedSpa has a "Not Ready to Book" path. Location selection often shapes navigation and booking (Milk + Honey). Upkeep keeps booking access "prominent and persistent". — [Workee, best med spa websites (reverified Jul 2026)](https://workee.ai/blog/best-medspa-websites)

**(e) Fitness / yoga / pilates**
- The goal of Barrel's Barry's redesign was to "streamline the customer journey for someone looking to book a class within their region", adding education touchpoints and Shopify merch. Barrel reports 17K orders/month and a $133 AOV after launch, with no baseline given. — [Barrel case study: Barry's](https://www.barrelny.com/work/barrys)
- Pilates: class booking is "front and center" with a multi-location flow (Pilates Republic). Brighton Pilates has a persistent "Book a Class" button in a fixed header, and Tropic State uses Momence integration. Exhale Pilates sells class packs and memberships through WooCommerce. — [Colorlib pilates examples, Oct 2026](https://colorlib.com/wp/pilates-studio-website-examples/)
- Yoga: intro offers are a key conversion ("Try a Free Week" at Yoga Pod Reno; "7 Classes for $120" at GOOD Pilates). Yoga on the Lane offers dual paths (Studio vs Online, each with Book), and Unplugged Yoga uses a 3-step onboarding. — [Zarla yoga examples, updated Sep 2026](https://www.zarla.com/guides/yoga-website-examples)

**(f) Physiotherapy**
- Every physio example reviewed includes or promotes online booking or scheduling. Professional PT organizes its site around "find the nearest center", and SPEAR uses clear CTAs plus online booking. — [Seahawk Media, physical therapist websites (Aug 2025, mod. Feb 2026)](https://seahawkmedia.com/design/physical-therapist-websites/)

### Inferences
- Synthesis, primary CTA per niche:
  - Medical: "Book appointment" plus phone, with a location/doctor finder for multi-site practices.
  - Dental: "Book" plus an emergency line and a new-patient offer.
  - Therapy: "Free 15-min consultation" plus an availability badge.
  - Salon/barber/nails: "Book now" that deep-links into Fresha, Vagaro or Booksy, plus walk-in info.
  - Med spa: "Book consultation" plus a location picker.
  - Gym: "Join / Free trial / Book class" plus the timetable.
  - Yoga/pilates: "Intro offer" plus the schedule.
  - Physio: "Book assessment" plus a conditions finder.
- Because older and less digitally confident users still prefer the phone (JMIR 2024), healthcare niches should pair the online-booking button with a tap-to-call button on mobile rather than replace it.
- Becker's "unsure what type of appointment they need" (7%) suggests a booking flow that starts from a symptom, concern or service category rather than from a raw calendar. Medical, physio and aesthetics are the best fits for this.

### Gaps
- No controlled A/B evidence was found comparing a sticky "Book" button with a non-sticky one for small practices. The persistent-button pattern rests on observed prevalence, not measured lift.
- No independent conversion data was found for fitness timetable widgets (Mindbody, Glofox, Momence) versus custom schedules.

## 2. Dominant layout and page patterns per niche (hero, menus/price lists, team, before/after, schedules, location/hours, FAQ, mobile)

### Takeaway
Most niches share the same skeleton: a hero with one CTA, a services grid, team/practitioner cards, reviews, a location and hours block, an FAQ and a footer CTA. The niches differ mainly in their signature module:
- Dental and aesthetics: before/after gallery.
- Therapy: availability, fees and specialisms checklist.
- Fitness: timetable or class finder plus intro offer.
- Salon, barber and nails: service menu, Instagram grid and walk-ins.
- Med spa: treatment taxonomy by concern, plus memberships and financing.
- Physio: conditions-treated pages and multi-location finder.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Medical**
- Framer clinic templates include doctor profiles, patient testimonials, an FAQ, service showcases, an appointment-request form and CMS-ready content. The pages are Home, About, Services, Contact and legal pages (APEX Clinic, which is "aimed at dental clinics but adaptable to physio, dermatology, pediatrics"). — [Framer Marketplace: APEX Clinic](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/apex-clinic/)
- Larger templates split services into department or specialty pages (Curely, 17 pages; MediPro, 14 pages with doctor profiles and case studies; MedExpert, 12 pages). Common traits are whitespace-heavy layouts, a clear heading hierarchy and staff bios with credentials. — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/healthcare-website-design-examples/)

**(b) Dental**
- Core modules: dentist profiles with photo, philosophy of care and credentials; a before/after or smile gallery reachable from the homepage; an emergency section; upfront pricing and financing calculators (Smile Direct Club); treatment comparisons (braces vs Invisalign vs aligners at Diamond Braces); a location finder for chains (Aspen Dental, Dental Care Alliance); and an office tour page (Beehive). — [Krishaweb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-dental-website-designs/)

**(c) Therapy**
- Hero types:
  - Single portrait plus name and tagline (jessicameiman.com).
  - Photo of the real empty armchair or office (michelleharwelltherapy.com).
  - Photo of the real playroom (childfamilytherapycollective.com).
  - Half-screen portrait (angierichey.com).
  - Cut-out portrait (blwtherapy.com).
  - Question-led headline (tampacounselingplace.com).
- Other modules:
  - Specialisms as a checklist (stateofmindkc.com).
  - Permanent "Rates" and "Availability" pages in the nav (zoeriggs.com).
  - Rates itemized by session length (millenniallifecounseling.com).
  - Insurance logos along the bottom (theempoweringspace.com).
  - Three clinics shown as plain text links (collective-psychology.com).
- One bespoke example (miljabrecherdemuro.com) uses no photography at all: black and white with a stacked service list. — [Colorlib therapist websites](https://colorlib.com/wp/therapist-websites/)

**(d) Salons / barbers / nails / spas / med spas**
- Barber heroes:
  - A client mid-cape (Long & Short).
  - A heritage crest wordmark with no photography (Master Barbers LA).
  - A neon sign over a dim interior (Backstage).
  - A tile mosaic with hours given their own tile (Maloney's).
  - A product hero (Barber Barber).
- The city name appears in the headline on most template examples. Prices are rarely shown: only Ironside & Crowe has a priced service menu up front. — [Zarla barber examples](https://www.zarla.com/guides/barbershop-website-examples)
- Nails:
  - Instagram feeds and grid galleries are common.
  - Animated marquee banners appear on several Squarespace sites (Varnish, Tropical Popical, NAF!).
  - Client-review marquees (DBCN).
  - A split layout with colour blocks (Ama The Salon).
  - A transparent pricing display (UL Nails & Spa).
  - A location finder for multi-site brands (QQ Nails, Honey Nails). — [Colorlib nail salons](https://colorlib.com/wp/nail-salon-website-examples/)
- Spas: service pages with pricing and treatment durations (North Pearl, Elements Massage), services plus deals (Crystal Clear), late-arrival and cancellation policies shown (Bodhi Spa), and nature-setting imagery and video (Refuge). — [GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/blog/spa-websites)
- Med spas: services grouped by category or concern, so a large menu can be scanned (VIO, Cienega); location-first navigation (Milk + Honey, JECT); memberships and offers (JECT); financing and specials (Doctor Qazi); before/after content (Milk + Honey, La Jolla Cosmetic Laser Clinic). — [Workee](https://workee.ai/blog/best-medspa-websites)

**(e) Fitness / yoga / pilates**
- Pilates:
  - Large hero sections with whitespace (Pilates Republic).
  - A split-layout hero gallery with icon-driven benefits (Pilates on 10th).
  - Scrolling marquees and overlapping image blocks (Gemini, Kefi, Page One).
  - A hero video (House of Sculpt).
  - Testimonials woven throughout (Pilates Lane).
  - In-person and virtual classes presented equally (Uden).
  - Class booking alongside retail (House of Sculpt, Scout Studios). — [Colorlib pilates](https://colorlib.com/wp/pilates-studio-website-examples/)
- Yoga: a "meet the team" row of instructor portraits near the top (Kind); classes named by outcome (Unplugged Yoga); a class schedule section plus a booking CTA plus an instructor section (Willow & Vale template); a gift-card prompt (Inna Bliss). — [Zarla yoga](https://www.zarla.com/guides/yoga-website-examples)
- Gyms: card entrance animations on scroll (Buckler); homepage video and imagery with hover animations (Equinox); hover menu animations (Sebastian Lundberg, a personal-trainer site). — [KIJO London, Apr 2026](https://kijo.london/blog/fitness-websites)

**(f) Physio / holistic**
- Services and locations are organized so visitors find the nearest centre (Professional PT). Other recurring modules are detailed treatment pages plus an evidence-based blog (Cioffredi), patient success stories and testimonials, and outcome metrics (Therapydia). Design stays consistent across multiple centres. — [Seahawk Media](https://seahawkmedia.com/design/physical-therapist-websites/)
- Framer "Healing" template for therapy, yoga, nutrition and wellness: earthy tones, soft visuals and a "warm, grounding feel". Spineo targets ortho, chiro and physio with clean lines and logical structure. — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/healthcare-website-design-examples/)

### Inferences
- Synthesis, recommended page order. Each is a starting skeleton for an AI builder, not a sourced standard.
  - **Medical clinic:** Header (logo, nav, phone, Book) → hero (headline, Book + Call, next-available or opening-hours chip) → services grid (icon cards) → doctors (photo, name, specialty, credentials, Book) → how-it-works/what-to-expect steps → reviews and accreditation strip → insurance/payment logos → location map + hours table + parking/transit → FAQ accordion → footer CTA. On mobile, a sticky bottom bar with [Call] and [Book].
  - **Dental:** Same skeleton, plus an emergency banner, new-patient offer, before/after slider or gallery, financing calculator and practice tour photos.
  - **Therapy:** Portrait or real-room hero → availability badge → "who I help" checklist → approach/modalities → fees and insurance (a block on the homepage or one click away) → FAQ (what a first session is like) → free-consultation CTA → in-person/telehealth location.
  - **Salon / barber / nails:** Full-bleed image or video hero + Book → service menu with prices and durations (tabs by category) → team/stylists with "book with X" → Instagram-style gallery grid → reviews → hours + map + walk-in policy → gift cards/shop.
  - **Med spa / aesthetic:** Editorial hero → "shop by concern" or treatment taxonomy → before/after gallery (filterable) → providers with credentials → memberships and financing → location selector → consultation booking.
  - **Gym / boutique fitness:** Video hero + "Free trial / Book first class" → class types → timetable widget (day tabs, filters by class, coach and location) → pricing/membership cards (3-tier) → coaches → transformation stories → locations → app download.
  - **Yoga / pilates:** Calm photo hero + intro offer → class types for beginners → weekly schedule → teachers row → pricing (drop-in, packs, unlimited) → studio photos → FAQ (what to bring).
  - **Physio:** Hero + "Book assessment" → conditions-treated grid (back, knee, sports, post-op) → treatment approach → clinicians → outcomes/testimonials → insurance and direct billing → locations.

### Gaps
- No study was found that quantifies whether price lists on salon or barber sites increase or decrease bookings. The roundups only observe that prices are rarely shown on barber sites.
- Timetable UI conventions (week grid vs day list vs filterable list) are not documented in any source fetched. The recommendation above is synthesis.

## 3. UI/UX best practices with evidence (NN/g, booking UX, accessibility for older and anxious users)

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is general rather than niche-specific:
- NN/g's older-adult research: larger text and targets, high contrast, forgiving inputs and errors.
- WCAG motion guidance: honour `prefers-reduced-motion` for vestibular-sensitive users.
- Booking studies: website ease of use strongly tracks online-booking uptake, and many patients still want the phone.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g, "Usability for Senior Citizens" (Lexie Kane, Sep 2019; older evidence, flagged):
  - Ability to use websites declines about 0.8% per year from ages 25 to 60, so age-related design needs begin well before 65.
  - Barriers: small text, tiny tap targets, low contrast, inflexible inputs, unforgiving errors.
  - Guidelines: avoid tiny or light text, use large click targets, accept multiple input formats (type a time rather than force a picker; accept hyphens and parentheses in phone numbers), tolerate typos, and place clear error messages where they are noticed.
  - The full report has 87 guidelines for users 65+. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/)
- WCAG 2.1 SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA): users must be able to disable non-essential, interaction-triggered motion. Parallax is the named example, and it can cause dizziness, nausea and headaches in people with vestibular disorders. Techniques: CSS and JS `prefers-reduced-motion` (C39, SCR40) and a user preference toggle. — [W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
- Online booking uptake is strongly associated with perceived website ease of use (OR 6.34). Barriers include in-person registration steps and older users' unfamiliarity. Recommendation: keep phone access. — [JMIR 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e51931)
- Login friction and platform ineffectiveness are among the reasons patients don't book online (3% and 8%). — [Becker's 2024](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/innovation/why-patients-dont-use-online-scheduling)
- Framer's healthcare roundup mentions accessibility (screen-reader-friendly scheduling in the HealthJoy template, WCAG alignment) and mobile-first design as recurring traits. — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/healthcare-website-design-examples/)
- For anxious dental patients, the advice is to balance clinical credibility with warmth and show faces and philosophy of care, not just qualifications. — [Krishaweb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-dental-website-designs/)
- Tend (dental) chose a warm, muted palette with rounded forms and hospitality cues from boutique hotels to reduce dental phobia, and kept the site's muted tones consistent with the clinic interior. — [SURFACE, Apr 2022](https://surfacemag.com/articles/tends-design-first-approach-makes-dentistry-nearly-phobia-free/)

### Inferences
- Synthesis, accessibility defaults for health niches (a–c and f):
  - Body text at least 16–18px.
  - Tap targets of 44–48px or more.
  - WCAG AA contrast at minimum, so avoid pale sage text on beige.
  - Tap-to-call on mobile.
  - Booking forms that accept free-format phone and date entry and don't require an account before choosing a slot.
  - All decorative motion switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
  - No autoplay video with sound.
  - No parallax on therapy or medical sites.
- For anxious users (therapy, dental, some medical): reduce uncertainty in the UI with "what to expect" step blocks, visible fees, availability status and photos of the real room and the real practitioner. The therapy roundup shows these are already prevalent.
- The low-contrast beige/sage palettes common in therapy and spa sites are an accessibility risk for older users. The builder should check contrast when it applies such palettes.

### Gaps
- I did not find a current (2023–2026) NN/g article specific to healthcare or provider websites in this session. The NN/g evidence used is the 2019 senior-citizens article. NN/g healthcare-specific findings (e.g., doctor-profile research) should be treated as unverified here.
- No peer-reviewed study was found on the visual design of therapist websites for anxious users. That guidance is practitioner or agency opinion.
- Baymard has "Health & Beauty" (2023) and "B2B Medical & Pharma" benchmarks ([Baymard H&B 2023](https://baymard.com/research-articles/health-and-beauty-benchmark-2023), [Baymard B2B medical](https://baymard.com/research/b2b-medical-pharma)). These are ecommerce-focused and not fetched, so they are not used as service-booking evidence.

## 4. Prototypical visual style per niche, mapped to style labels

### Takeaway
- Medical: clean minimalist or flat, white with blue/teal (the "clinical" default), moving toward warm hospitality-minimal at premium brands.
- Dental: the same clinical default, with a design-led warm-pastel minority.
- Therapy: organic, soft minimalism: cream, sage, terracotta, dusty pink, serif headings, portrait photography.
- Barbers: dark heritage (black + gold, crests).
- Nail studios: minimalist white or pastel-pink, with Instagram grids.
- Spas and med spas: luxury minimal or editorial, with neutral palettes and delicate serifs.
- Gyms: dark, bold, high-contrast with video.
- Yoga and pilates: organic or earthy minimal, sometimes Y2K-pastel or editorial at boutique brands.
- Physio: clean clinical-sporty with calm palettes.

### Cited Findings
**(c) Therapy, palette evidence (29 sites with hex codes).**
- Most sites are white or cream bases with muted accents:
  - Sage (#9EACAD, collective-psychology.com).
  - Deep forest green (#355333, #0D352D).
  - Taupe and dried florals with serif type (jareebasgall.com).
  - Terracotta bands (#894224, #E57B53, dltwellcollective.com).
  - Dusty pink and peach (everbetherapy.com).
  - Cream with deep teal and blush (cindyshutherapy.com).
- Outliers: black-and-white typographic with no photos (miljabrecherdemuro.com), near-black with a single deep-red accent and an irreverent tone (therapywithjoy.com), deep purple with marigolds (embracecorazon.com) and a navy plus dusty rose "more corporate" look (attunedpsychology.com).
- Platforms are mostly Squarespace, then WordPress, Wix and Webflow. — [Colorlib therapist websites](https://colorlib.com/wp/therapist-websites/)

**(d) Barbers.** Most entries use black, charcoal or navy with gold, brass or another accent. Heritage cues come from crests, serifs and neon scripts. There are light or playful outliers: a cream serif shave parlour, a bright-blue fade specialist and an orange-mint kids' barber. — [Zarla](https://www.zarla.com/guides/barbershop-website-examples)

**(d) Nails.**
- The most common look is minimalist and whitespace-heavy, followed by pastel or pink-and-white palettes and luxury black-and-white schemes.
- Editorial or luxury examples: Paintbox (black and white, serif display type freight-display-pro, editorial storytelling), JINsoon (luxury black-and-white minimalism) and Paint Nail Bar (Avenir, Didot, DIN Next).
- Squarespace powers 14 of the 26 sites. — [Colorlib nails](https://colorlib.com/wp/nail-salon-website-examples/)

**(d) Spas.** Calming, soft neutral palettes and clean minimalist layouts. Linnaean (London) uses "delicate fonts, muted colors, elegant feel". Refuge uses nature imagery and video. — [GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/blog/spa-websites)

**(e) Gyms.**
- Luxury gym examples: a "stripped back, dark colour palette" (Buckler, Brazil) and a "minimal, luxury palette" with strong photo and video (Equinox).
- Fuoripista (Italy) uses large elegant type with minimal layout.
- Sebastian Lundberg (Denmark, a personal trainer) uses impactful type with bright accent pops on a classic layout. — [KIJO London](https://kijo.london/blog/fitness-websites)

**(e) Yoga.**
- Earthy palettes suit calm, small studios: Kind uses chocolate and cream with soft lowercase type, and Unplugged uses cream and teal with serif and script.
- Dark or illustrated styles target other audiences: Y7 Studio (NYC) is black and orange with graffiti-adjacent display type and a streetwear feel, and Yoga on the Lane uses bold multicolour illustrations instead of photos. — [Zarla yoga](https://www.zarla.com/guides/yoga-website-examples)

**(e) Pilates.**
- Earthy or warm tones: Pilates Lane, Essential MVMT, Tribe.
- Cream, black and sage: House of Sculpt.
- Bold lime accent: Prescription.
- Lime, apricot and lilac: Rebalance.
- Peachy-orange and cream with sparkle graphics, Y2K-adjacent: Kink Pilates.
- Editorial: Be Kind Studios.
- Marquee banners are frequent.
- Squarespace is dominant (19 of 26). — [Colorlib pilates](https://colorlib.com/wp/pilates-studio-website-examples/)

**(f) Physio.** Branding ranges from minimalist to bold, with calming palettes for reassurance. Cioffredi has a calming colour scheme and readable type. RISE pairs bold branding with a calm overall feel. SPEAR is bold and sports-focused. — [Seahawk](https://seahawkmedia.com/design/physical-therapist-websites/)

**(a) Medical, premium direction.**
- One Medical's brand guidelines call for a custom serif headline face (One GT Super) paired with Ginto sans.
- Photography should be intimate and tightly cropped, showing people together rather than office spaces.
- Editorial illustration should use earthy hues, natural textures and curved organic forms, avoiding markers of a specific era.
- Tone: "human-centered, considered, modern, and premium, yet approachable." — [One Medical brand guidelines](https://www.onemedical.com/brand-guidelines/)
- Framer's medical templates mostly use clean, whitespace-heavy layouts. MedExpert uses cool accent tones, HealthJoy a bright palette, and Healing (wellness) earthy tones. — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/healthcare-website-design-examples/)
- Webflow's own medical category page uses a bright blue theme colour (#146ef5). This is weak evidence: it is Webflow's brand colour, not a template's. — [Webflow](https://webflow.com/templates/category/medical-websites)

### Inferences
- **Synthesis, prototype vs differentiator style map.** The prototype column is what most sites look like; the differentiator column shows proven alternatives.
  - **Medical clinic:** Prototype is Flat/Material or Minimalist, clinical white plus blue/teal, sans-serif, stock doctor photos, icon service grid. Differentiators are hospitality-minimal or editorial (custom serif + sans, warm neutrals, intimate photography, organic illustration, as at One Medical, Parsley and Tia) and Scandinavian/Japandi calm.
  - **Dental:** Prototype is the clinical white/blue, smile-photo hero with before/after slider. Differentiators are warm pastel "design-led" hospitality-minimal (Tend) and Bento-style service grids. Bold, playful brand concepts can also work (Beehive's single concept is cited as outperforming generic polish).
  - **Therapy:** Prototype is Organic or soft Minimalist with cream, sage, terracotta and dusty pink, serif or script headings, a portrait, lots of whitespace, plus Squarespace defaults. Differentiators are Typographic-led black and white (Brecher), dark single-accent with an irreverent tone (Therapy With Joy), and culturally specific maximal colour (Embrace Corazón).
  - **Hair salon:** Prototype is Luxury or Editorial minimal (neutral, serif display, large photography). Differentiators are Collage and Maximalist (fashion-forward salons) and Kinetic marquee type.
  - **Barber:** Prototype is Dark heritage/Retro (black + gold, crest, serif or script, neon). Differentiators are Neo-brutalist or bold-colour (acid yellow at Barber Barber) and light shave-parlour Retro.
  - **Nails:** Prototype is Minimalist white or pastel pink with an Instagram grid. Differentiators are Y2K and pastel gradients (NAF!, Cute Nail Studio), Editorial black and white (Paintbox) and tropical Maximalist (Tropical Popical).
  - **Spa:** Prototype is Luxury and Organic (muted neutrals, delicate serif, nature video). Differentiators are Wabi-sabi and Japandi textures, and Immersive scroll used sparingly.
  - **Med spa / aesthetic:** Prototype is Luxury minimal (white/blush/beige, serif, glossy model photography). Differentiators are Editorial magazine-style "shop by concern" layouts and Bento treatment grids.
  - **Gym:** Prototype is Dark/tech, bold condensed sans in all caps, a full-bleed video hero and neon or red accents. Differentiators are Luxury minimal (Equinox), Kinetic typography and Neo-brutalist.
  - **Boutique fitness:** Prototype is Dark bold or brand-colour bold. Differentiators are Editorial and Y2K pastel.
  - **Yoga / pilates:** Prototype is Organic or earthy Minimalist (cream, sage, terracotta, serif, marquee banners). Differentiators are Dark streetwear (Y7), Illustrative (Yoga on the Lane), Y2K pastel (Kink), Editorial (Be Kind) and bold lime/lilac (Rebalance).
  - **Physio:** Prototype is clean Flat/Minimal (blue, teal or green), with sporty bold accents for sports rehab. Differentiators are bold sports-brand Dark (SPEAR-like) and calm Scandinavian.
  - **Holistic / alternative wellness:** Prototype is Organic (earth tones, botanical imagery, soft shapes). Differentiators are Wabi-sabi, Japandi and Illustrative.
- **Synthesis, label applicability:** Glassmorphism, Neumorphism and Claymorphism appear in health-app UI kits on Dribbble but were not observed in any real practice-site roundup in this research. Treat them as rare and low-fit for practice websites (contrast problems with neumorphism are a known accessibility issue, but that is not sourced here). Brutalist proper was not observed outside fashion-adjacent salons and gyms.

### Gaps
- No quantitative census of colours across thousands of medical or dental sites was found. The "clinical white/blue" prototype is supported only indirectly (template features and practitioner commentary) and by common knowledge. Treat it as a strong but unquantified prior.
- I had very few non-English-market examples (Brazil: Buckler; Italy: Fuoripista; Denmark: Sebastian Lundberg; UK: Linnaean, Bupa, Brighton Pilates; Australia/NZ: several pilates and yoga sites; South Africa: Legends; Shanghai: an interior only). There is no Japan, Korea, DACH or LatAm medical or dental site evidence. This is a gap for the "global view".

## 5. Styles that differentiate successfully, and styles to avoid

### Takeaway
Hospitality-inspired, warm and design-led branding is the proven differentiator in healthcare and dental: Tend, One Medical, Parsley, Tia, Oula. In fitness and yoga, strong brand worlds (dark streetwear, luxury minimal, bold colour) differentiate. Motion-heavy, parallax and autoplay-heavy styles carry documented accessibility risk for vestibular-sensitive users, and they work against the calm that health and therapy visitors need.

### Cited Findings
- **Tend (US dental):** a warm, muted palette, smooth rounded forms and boutique-hotel and restaurant hospitality cues, aimed at making dentistry "nearly phobia-free". The website's muted tones match the clinic. — [SURFACE 2022](https://surfacemag.com/articles/tends-design-first-approach-makes-dentistry-nearly-phobia-free/). Krishaweb (2026) also lists hellotend.com first as "warm, modern, deliberately non-clinical" with spa-like photography and a "prominent and simple" booking CTA. — [Krishaweb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-dental-website-designs/). Tend also has a Red Dot entry (not fetched; year unverified) — [Red Dot](https://red-dot.org/project/tend-49427)
- **Beehive Dental:** a single brand concept applied consistently is cited as outperforming generic polish. — [Krishaweb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-dental-website-designs/)
- **Boutique healthcare with a hospitality lens (interiors, 2021, older evidence):**
  - Parsley Health: dusty desert hues, Pacific blue, rattan and plants (Alda Ly).
  - Oula (maternity): terracotta, soft pink, yellow and blue, curved forms.
  - Tia (women's health): white oak, terrazzo, blue accents, a living room instead of a waiting room (Rockwell Group LAB).
  - The sites are parsleyhealth.com, oulahealth.com and asktia.com. — [Hospitality Design, Jul 2021](https://hospitalitydesign.com/projects/boutique-healthcare/121536)
- **One Medical:** a premium, human-centred system with a custom serif, organic illustration and intimate photography. — [One Medical brand guidelines](https://www.onemedical.com/brand-guidelines/)
- **Y7 Studio:** differentiates yoga as dark, high-energy and streetwear rather than spiritual. — [Zarla yoga](https://www.zarla.com/guides/yoga-website-examples)
- **Equinox and Buckler:** differentiate through luxury minimal, dark palettes and premium photo and video. — [KIJO London](https://kijo.london/blog/fitness-websites)
- **Styles to avoid or temper:** parallax and other interaction-triggered motion can cause dizziness, nausea and headaches in people with vestibular disorders, and WCAG requires a way to disable it (AAA). — [W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
- Older users struggle with small, light text and low contrast. — [NN/g 2019](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/)
- Somatic Therapy Tampa is noted as having "the busiest palette in the set", an outlier among therapy sites. — [Colorlib therapist](https://colorlib.com/wp/therapist-websites/)

### Inferences
- **Synthesis, avoid list by niche:**
  - Therapy and medical: immersive scroll, scroll-jacking, parallax, kinetic type, autoplay video, dark/tech palettes and glitchy Y2K effects. These raise arousal and cognitive load for anxious or older users.
  - Dental and medical: low-contrast neumorphism or glassmorphism over photos (legibility risk).
  - Gyms: purely dark sites with thin grey text (contrast failures).
  - Spas and therapy: beige-on-beige low-contrast text.
  - Every niche: hiding prices or booking behind heavy animation.
- **Synthesis, where motion is fine:** gyms, boutique fitness, barbers and fashion-forward salons can carry kinetic typography, marquees, video heroes and scroll-triggered card reveals (Buckler, Equinox hover). Use them with `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks, and never let motion delay the Book CTA.
- "Design-led" differentiation in healthcare seems to work best when the website matches a physical hospitality-style clinic, as with Tend and Parsley. A small practice copying the aesthetic without the matching experience may create a mismatch. This is a hypothesis and is unsourced.

### Gaps
- No measured conversion or retention outcome was found for "design-led" health brands versus clinical-default sites. The evidence for differentiation is press, awards and practitioner opinion.
- No empirical study was found showing that dark gym sites convert better or worse than light ones.

## 6. Trust UI components per niche

### Takeaway
Trust is carried by people (real practitioner photos, credentials, philosophy), social proof (recent reviews, testimonials, outcome stories, before/after), money transparency (fees, insurance and financing logos, memberships) and operational signals (availability status, hours, policies, hygiene or safety protocols).

### Cited Findings
- **Dental:** dentist profiles with photo, credentials and philosophy of care; recent reviews as the main local trust signal; before/after galleries; upfront pricing and insurance billing; honest treatment comparisons (Smile Generation, Beehive, Diamond Braces). — [Krishaweb](https://www.krishaweb.com/blog/best-dental-website-designs/)
- **Medical templates:** staff bios, testimonials, reviews and credentials appear repeatedly. Vitalflow embeds reviews and staff photos. — [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/healthcare-website-design-examples/)
- **Therapy:**
  - Insurer and insurance logos (theempoweringspace.com, deepeddypsychotherapy.com).
  - A Zencare badge (blwtherapy.com).
  - Availability banners.
  - Fees on the homepage or one click away, and an itemized intake fee (dltwellcollective.com).
  - Telehealth plus in-person shown (attunedpsychology.com).
  - Real office or room photography. — [Colorlib therapist](https://colorlib.com/wp/therapist-websites/)
- **Spas:** COVID-19 protocols displayed (Forever Young Spa, an older-era hygiene signal), cancellation and late policies shown (Bodhi), and reviews and testimonials highlighted (Aura Medspa, La Belle). — [GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/blog/spa-websites)
- **Nails:** review marquees (DBCN), Okendo reviews (MiniLuxe), a testimonials section (UL Nails), and links to Yelp, Instagram and TikTok (Nail Lab). — [Colorlib nails](https://colorlib.com/wp/nail-salon-website-examples/)
- **Med spas:** provider and team info, payment options and financing (Tribeca, Doctor Qazi), before/after (Milk + Honey, La Jolla). — [Workee](https://workee.ai/blog/best-medspa-websites)
- **Physio:** patient success stories, testimonials, outcome metrics (Therapydia) and evidence-based education content (Cioffredi). — [Seahawk](https://seahawkmedia.com/design/physical-therapist-websites/)
- **Yoga:** named testimonials, diverse imagery and a team-first layout. — [Zarla yoga](https://www.zarla.com/guides/yoga-website-examples)
- **Barbers:** heritage signals ("Celebrating 10 Years" ticker at Master Barbers LA; "Traditional Family Barbers" at Gould). — [Zarla barber](https://www.zarla.com/guides/barbershop-website-examples)

### Inferences
- Synthesis, trust block kit an AI builder can insert:
  1. Credentials strip (degrees, registrations, association logos).
  2. Review summary card (star average, count, source, with 3 recent quotes).
  3. Insurance and payment logo row.
  4. Before/after slider with consent note (dental, aesthetics, hair).
  5. "Meet the team" cards with real photos.
  6. Hygiene and safety block (sterilization, single-use tools; especially nails, aesthetics, tattoo-adjacent).
  7. Policy accordion (cancellation, late arrival, deposits).
  8. Availability badge (therapy, or "next available slot" in clinics).
  9. Years-in-business or "est." badge (barbers, salons).
  10. Outcome metric tiles (physio, fitness).

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence was found on the effect of specific trust badges (e.g., association logos) on booking rates in these niches.

## 7. Prototypicality evidence from template marketplaces and builders

### Takeaway
Builders segment health and beauty into stable sub-categories (clinic, doctor, hospital, dentist; hair and beauty; spa; fitness). Best-selling themes are clinic, spa, hair and gym themes. Platform choice is niche-skewed: Squarespace dominates therapy, pilates and nail sites in the roundups, and dedicated booking platforms (GlossGenius, Fresha, Vagaro, Mindbody-type tools) shape salon and spa sites.

### Cited Findings
- **ThemeForest WordPress "Health & Beauty":** 900+ items. The top sellers shown were MediCenter (Health Medical Clinic, 12.7K sales), Jacqueline (Spa & Massage Salon, 5.8K), Curly (Hairdressers/Hair Salons, 5.2K) and GymBase (Gym Fitness, 4.9K). Barber and dental appear as popular search terms. — [ThemeForest](https://themeforest.net/category/wordpress/retail/health-beauty)
- **Webflow medical template sub-categories:** Clinic & Pharmacy, Doctor, Hospital, Dentist, Veterinary, plus a separate Hair & Beauty category. Therapy is not a separate category. The page showed "No items found" when fetched, possibly a render issue. — [Webflow](https://webflow.com/templates/category/medical-websites)
- **Framer Marketplace:** many free dental and clinic templates: APEX Clinic, Clireo Healthcare, ReoDental, Dentalio, Harboreview Clinic, Curadent, DentaCare, DentistryX. Framer's own roundup covers 15 healthcare templates (Curely, MediPro, Al-Medi, Vitalflow, Enima, MedExpert, HealthJoy, Healing, Holicare, Spineo, Doctr X, Mediso, Omnis, OraCare). The shared traits are clean, whitespace-heavy layouts, booking-first CTAs, trust signals and niche targeting. — [Framer APEX Clinic](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/apex-clinic/); [Framer blog](https://www.framer.com/blog/healthcare-website-design-examples/); [Framer dental search results](https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/templates/dental/)
- **Platform share in roundups:**
  - Squarespace: 14 of 26 nail sites, 19 of 26 pilates sites, and the most common platform in the therapy list (with WordPress, Wix, Webflow and bespoke).
  - GlossGenius: hosts many spa examples, on subdomains with built-in booking. — [Colorlib nails](https://colorlib.com/wp/nail-salon-website-examples/); [Colorlib pilates](https://colorlib.com/wp/pilates-studio-website-examples/); [Colorlib therapist](https://colorlib.com/wp/therapist-websites/); [GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/blog/spa-websites)
- **Wix Marketplace** has spa templates such as "Lumière Spa" (not fetched). — [Wix template](https://www.wix.com/marketplace/template/03eb19db-b760-46f1-9147-376f8f975c41)
- **Zarla** publishes niche templates for barbers (Ironside & Crowe, Beacon & Blade, Fade Theory, Crown & Kings), yoga (Willow & Vale) and others. They mirror the patterns of real sites: a dark showcase hero for barbers, a full-width overlay hero with schedule and team for yoga. — [Zarla barber](https://www.zarla.com/guides/barbershop-website-examples); [Zarla yoga](https://www.zarla.com/guides/yoga-website-examples)

### Inferences
- The predominance of Squarespace in therapy, pilates and nails likely explains much of the prototypical look: serif/sans pairings, generous whitespace, marquee blocks and full-bleed image heroes. An AI builder's "prototype" output for these niches will read as "Squarespace-ish". To differentiate, it needs to break those defaults deliberately.
- Fitness and salon prototypes are shaped partly by embedded booking widgets (Mindbody, Momence, Fresha, Vagaro), whose own UI often dominates the schedule or booking section. The builder should style around the embed or deep-link to it.

### Gaps
- Exact template counts per category for Webflow, Squarespace and Wix could not be retrieved (Webflow rendered empty). ThemeForest's count is for the whole Health & Beauty category, not per niche.
- No visual analysis of ThemeForest preview images was possible (text-only fetch).
